A fuller game, gently.

Japanese Riichi · four players · East-round match

Start with 13 tiles. On your turn, draw one and discard one. Most winning hands have four sets and a pair. A set is a run of three consecutive numbers in one suit, three identical tiles, or a declared group of four.

Chi: take the player on your left’s discard to complete a run. Pon: take any opponent’s discard to complete three of a kind. Kan: declare four of a kind, then take a replacement tile. These buttons appear only when legal.

Riichi: commit a closed, ready hand and place a 1,000-point bet. After that, your hand is locked and you normally discard each new draw. Tsumo wins on your draw. Ron wins on a discard.

A matching shape alone is not always enough: Riichi requires a yaku. All simples, a dragon triplet, riichi, and a closed self-draw are examples. Dora add bonus points but do not count as a yaku. You cannot ron while furiten, including when your own discards contain one of your winning tiles.

The engine checks calls, turn order, yaku, furiten, special hands, the dead wall, dora and red fives, exhaustive and abortive draws, dealer repeats, point payments, and match results. There are no countdowns. The helper’s distance measures tile shape, not guaranteed scoring or a guaranteed number of turns.

Draft scope: practice against three computer players, or two people online with two computer players. Online matches use your existing room code and saved player seat. Either person can close the app between choices. Calls may wait for the other person to respond.
